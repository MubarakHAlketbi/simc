BESTIAL WRATH
The most important aspect is that you use  Bestial Wrath on cooldown, as often as you possibly can. It sounds simple but losing one use loses a lot of damage.
While there are a lot of talents that interact with  Bestial Wrath, most of them are passive and not worth playing around.
 Bloodshed will be applied to the target the pet is currently attacking. If your pet is not attacking anything, you will not get a bleed.
 Nature's Ally will make your  Kill Command more powerful, indirectly, since there is one additional pet casting it.
The initial hit of  Bestial Wrath is fairly large and more importantly, affected by  Beast Cleave. When combined with  Nature's Ally, this makes every individual additional hit of  Bestial Wrath trigger their own  Beast Cleave.

You will cause your next  Kill Command to trigger a  Stampede!
This will fire a line of animals 30 yards in front of you. These animals have no height and will not adjust for curves in the terrain. This means they miss very easily.
For  Dark Ranger:
You will gain one  Deathblow.
You will gain  Withering Fire, making your  Black Arrows more powerful for a short period.
 Wailing Dead summons one Dark Hound.
This Hound, will, notably, trigger  Pact of the Hollow, making  Kill Command notably stronger.
The Dark Hound will do a lot of damage and also benefits from  Dire Cleave. This can turn  Bestial Wrath into an AoE burst cooldown at the cost of only one talent point.
 Wailing Dead also gives you access to  Wailing Arrow, which can be used both for its large damage and the AoE silence effect.

PERFECTING YOUR COOLDOWN WINDOW
As you approach your cooldown window, you should start planning towards having two charges of  Kill Command available. If you are  Dark Ranger, make sure you do not have any  Deathblow active as  Withering Fire will give one for you.
Make sure your pet is attacking your current priority target, so that  Bloodshed lasts its full duration. If your pet is not attacking anything, you do not get a bleed.
Check out the UI & Macros page for pet attack macros.
Any item that boosts your direct damage (such as a potion or a trinket that gives any stat but haste) should be active before  Bestial Wrath strikes the target, so that it benefits.
Macroing them together is fine for this purpose.
On AoE, make sure that you have  Beast Cleave active when you press  Bestial Wrath, so you get a big AoE burst.
During the cooldown itself, you should aim to spend as many  Kill Commands as possible, and buff them all with  Nature's Ally.
Since your cooldown is so short, you might get it back towards the end of Mythic+ packs. It might be wise to hold it if you don't anticipate the encounter lasting much longer.
For  Pack Leader:
You should think about where you are standing relative to the enemy as you go into your cooldown window. You should aim to be at a similar height as the enemy, and not too far away.
If the enemy is about to die, or move, it might be worth delaying your cooldown window.
If using  Killer Cobra , you should look to prioritize  Cobra Shot over  Barbed Shot during  Bestial Wrath.
For  Dark Ranger:
The Dark Hound with  Dire Cleave causes your cooldown to gain a powerful AoE burst component. Take advantage of this in raid when not running a full AoE setup, and line it up with important add spawns.
Note that the priority changes when  Withering Fire fades and  Bestial Wrath is still active. During this small period, you should aim to use  Barbed Shot over  Black Arrow.
 Wailing Arrow is generally flexible in how you use it but you should generally avoid wasting resources while using it, such as  Kill Command reaching two charges. You should, however, always use it before the effect runs out. You also have the option of taking advantage of silence effect in Mythic+.

Note that  Algeth'ar Puzzle Box has a two second cast time. This means that you want to use it two seconds before the encounter starts, and two seconds before  Bestial Wrath is available.

For Potions:
You should use your potion alongside your  Bestial Wrath. Note that it needs to be active before the initial hit so that too gets boosted. Macro-ing it to  Bestial Wrath works for this.
You should look to use your potions alongside your trinkets or racials, as long as it does not cost you a usage of your potion.
For Racials:
Any racial that increases your stats, such as  Berserking,  Ancestral Call,  Fireblood and  Blood Fury, should be used alongside  Bestial Wrath.
You should look to use your racial alongside your trinkets or potion, as long as it does not cost you a usage of your racial.

Any trinket that increases your stats, including as  Light Company Guidon,  Void Execution Mandate,  Algeth'ar Puzzle Box,  Emberwing Feather and  Freightrunner's Flask, should be used alongside  Bestial Wrath.
Note that  Algeth'ar Puzzle Box has a two second cast time. This means that you want to use it two seconds before the encounter starts, and two seconds before  Bestial Wrath is available.
Any trinket that simply does damage, should just be used on cooldown, such as  Latch's Crooked Hook,  Ranger-Captain's Iridescent Insignia and  Void Stalker's Contract.
For Potions:
You should use your potion alongside your  Bestial Wrath. Note that it needs to be active before the initial hit so that too gets boosted. Macro-ing it to  Bestial Wrath works for this.
You should look to use your potions alongside your trinkets or racials, as long as it does not cost you a usage of your potion.
For Racials:
Any racial that increases your stats, such as  Berserking,  Ancestral Call,  Fireblood and  Blood Fury, should be used alongside  Bestial Wrath.
You should look to use your racial alongside your trinkets or potion, as long as it does not cost you a usage of your racial.
Any other racial should not be used rotationally, you would rather use a  Cobra Shot.

PERFECTING YOUR COOLDOWN WINDOW
As you approach your cooldown window, you should start planning towards having two charges of  Kill Command available. If you are  Dark Ranger, make sure you do not have any  Deathblow active as  Withering Fire will give one for you.
Make sure your pet is attacking your current priority target, so that  Bloodshed lasts its full duration. If your pet is not attacking anything, you do not get a bleed.
Check out the UI & Macros page for pet attack macros.
Any item that boosts your direct damage (such as a potion or a trinket that gives any stat but haste) should be active before  Bestial Wrath strikes the target, so that it benefits.
Macroing them together is fine for this purpose.
On AoE, make sure that you have  Beast Cleave active when you press  Bestial Wrath, so you get a big AoE burst.
During the cooldown itself, you should aim to spend as many  Kill Commands as possible, and buff them all with  Nature's Ally.
Since your cooldown is so short, you might get it back towards the end of Mythic+ packs. It might be wise to hold it if you don't anticipate the encounter lasting much longer.
For  Pack Leader:
You should think about where you are standing relative to the enemy as you go into your cooldown window. You should aim to be at a similar height as the enemy, and not too far away.
If the enemy is about to die, or move, it might be worth delaying your cooldown window.
If using  Killer Cobra , you should look to prioritize  Cobra Shot over  Barbed Shot during  Bestial Wrath.
For  Dark Ranger:
The Dark Hound with  Dire Cleave causes your cooldown to gain a powerful AoE burst component. Take advantage of this in raid when not running a full AoE setup, and line it up with important add spawns.
Note that the priority changes when  Withering Fire fades and  Bestial Wrath is still active. During this small period, you should aim to use  Barbed Shot over  Black Arrow.
 Wailing Arrow is generally flexible in how you use it but you should generally avoid wasting resources while using it, such as  Kill Command reaching two charges. You should, however, always use it before the effect runs out. You also have the option of taking advantage of silence effect in Mythic+.

PET MANAGEMENT
Pets are notoriously awkward to manage, so this section will cover various tips and tricks to make this task easier and more effective.
For AoE, it's relatively important that your pet(s) are central in the pack to maximize the amount of targets hit by  Beast Cleave and  Wild Thrash. Pets will generally try to attack the back of the mob, so try to move them to target an enemy whose back is towards the middle of the pack.
The UI & Macros page has various tools to help control your pets better, including a solution to their broken autocast and various pet attack macros.
Pets like to get stuck a lot! Generally speaking, your best solution to this is either using your pets' "Follow" or "Passive" commands, or swapping between them. Make sure you have these bound to reasonable keys, as each problem may have a different solution. In a worst-case scenario, you will have to dismiss your pet and resummon it. On some encounters, your pet may die from improperly coded AoE, and on others, they may move out of range and despawn.
Your  Kill Command and  Barbed Shot will cause your pet charge towards the target. This is important to keep in mind when target-swapping, as you will want to make good use of these effectively to minimize your pets' downtime.
Your pet also has  Dash, which you can manually use to move it around quickly. It is set to auto-cast by default, which often wastes it.
It's worth turning off  Spirit Walk auto-cast, as it makes your pet start attacking the enemy slower.

PET MANAGEMENT
Pets are notoriously awkward to manage, so this section will cover various tips and tricks to make this task easier and more effective.
For AoE, it's relatively important that your pet(s) are central in the pack to maximize the amount of targets hit by  Beast Cleave and  Wild Thrash. Pets will generally try to attack the back of the mob, so try to move them to target an enemy whose back is towards the middle of the pack.
The UI & Macros page has various tools to help control your pets better, including a solution to their broken autocast and various pet attack macros.
Pets like to get stuck a lot! Generally speaking, your best solution to this is either using your pets' "Follow" or "Passive" commands, or swapping between them. Make sure you have these bound to reasonable keys, as each problem may have a different solution. In a worst-case scenario, you will have to dismiss your pet and resummon it. On some encounters, your pet may die from improperly coded AoE, and on others, they may move out of range and despawn.
Your  Kill Command and  Barbed Shot will cause your pet charge towards the target. This is important to keep in mind when target-swapping, as you will want to make good use of these effectively to minimize your pets' downtime.
Your pet also has  Dash, which you can manually use to move it around quickly. It is set to auto-cast by default, which often wastes it.
It's worth turning off  Spirit Walk auto-cast, as it makes your pet start attacking the enemy slower.
