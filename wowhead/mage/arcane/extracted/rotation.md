ARCANE SURGE
 Arcane Surge is simple, you use it, it spends all of your mana, does a burst of damage that hits 5 targets, then square root damage beyond that.  Arcane Surge then buffs you for 15-seconds, granting 35% additional spell damage and 425% mana regen. There are other properties of this that come from hero talents.
 Spellslinger
Spellslinger changes  Arcane Surge in one major way,  Splinterstorm in Midnight causes your Surge hit to generate 8x  Arcane Splinters and doubles Splinter generation during its buff;  Archmage's Wrath also buffs Surge damage a decent chunk. Keep in mind that Splinters for Spellslinger are fuel for the fire, it's what makes Spellslinger work.
 Sunfury
Sunfury has a few ways that it alters  Arcane Surge. First, when you cast Surge, any  Spellfire Spheres you have are consumed and added to your Surge buff for the duration of Surge. Second, when Surge ends, you gain  Arcane Soul and  Ashes of Inspiration, enabling a powerful burst of damage, ultimately extending your burst window; there is also a talent, though we won't take it for now, that can extend Surge's window a couple seconds.  Arcane Soul is a major reason why we cast  Touch of the Magi late in our Surge window as Sunfury. Lastly, when you cast Surge, you spawn an  Arcane Phoenix, which, for every Spellfire Sphere you consumed, cast empowered spells and the bird will also cast regular spells too. The bird is really complex in how it works, it detects whether it's single target or AOE and has a different action list based on this, it can cast it's own version of the following spells:

OPTIMIZING DAMAGE AS ARCANE
Some key things to note in general about good gameplay that will make you stand out;
Optimize around  Arcane Salvo; try to think about your next few seconds of gameplay, will adds spawn that need to die fast? Will the adds that are up now die soon? Consider saving and not saving around the current conditions - more adds generally means more damage overall and to your main target, thanks to  Resonance.
Don't allow yourself to get frazzled with mechanics, when movement is required, try to react quickly and consider your  Shimmer path and destination, ensure that you use Stutter Stepping in conjunction with Shimmer to minimize your downtime when handling mechanics.
 Prismatic Barrier is a must-cast defensive. Mages are not as tanky as we used to be, and Barrier spells received a sizable buff to both their quality and to their cast time - they only require 0.5 seconds now to cast, so there's no excuse - you can't deal damage if you're dead!
 Alter Time is a strong defensive, but don't be afraid to also double tap it for mobility in the moment. This can help you keep up with classes that have much better sustained mobility.
Your cooldown window only needs about 18 seconds for  Spellslinger and a maximum of 26 seconds for  Sunfury, and 18s for your miniburns; don't hold your cooldowns for too long if you can help it, remember how short the cooldown on  Touch of the Magi and  Arcane Surge are!

BEST ARCANE MAGE OPENER
Arcane Mage openers and how you use your cooldowns is entirely dictated by hero talent choice, with Spellslinger having a more conventional opener with an optional pooling opener and then using cooldowns and Sunfury being more exotic by using  Touch of the Magi later on during  Arcane Surge in order to capture the  Arcane Soul window after. No other choices you make have any impact on how you use your cooldowns. Sunfury doesn't pool resources on openers because you're basically doing that during Surge instead.
OPENER SEQUENCE
Resource Pooling
Arcane Pulse (4+ targets)
Remember that  Time Warp is a group cooldown, you should always ask someone if you're not sure when this is best used for your group and coordinate with your teammates for optimal use. If you don't care about their opinions or you are solo, just use it whenever you think you'll make good use of it!
Cast  Arcane Blast 2 seconds before pulling.
Cast  Arcane Orb and  Time Warp.
Cast  Arcane Blast until you have 20 stacks of  Arcane Salvo.
Use any potions, items, or trinkets that will buff you for more than 15 seconds.
Cast  Arcane Surge.
Use any potions, items, or trinkets that will buff you for less than or equal to 15 seconds.
Cast  Arcane Barrage and then  Touch of the Magi while it is mid-air.
45-seconds later, when  Touch of the Magis cooldown finishes, repeat the last step.

PRE-COMBAT CHECK
Before any combat happens, it is important for Arcane Mages to make sure the following steps are checked. This applies to raids, dungeons, delves, or even open-world content, these are the things that keep you powerful, make a habit of just using them when out of combat all the time!

Cast Group Buffs - Use  Arcane Intellect to buff you and your party with additional Intellect, healers love you, Warlocks wish they were you, and melee fear you.
Double Check Familiar -  Arcane Familiar has a tendancy to wander off sometimes, double check that he's with you. To be serious,  Arcane Familiar despawns from load screens, even though  Arcane Intellect does not, so you have to pay extra attention to ensure you have it. There are addons to help this in the UI section.
Apply Your Barrier -  Prismatic Barrier is insanely effective and having it up being combat just makes life easier and cost us nothing!
Is Aggro a Concern? - If you're at all concerned about aggro you should talent into  Mirror Image and use them before the pull. They don't do any damage but they make it so you generate 0 threat while they're active, allowing you to go crazy without upsetting your tank.

Arcane Surge then buffs you for 15-seconds, granting 35% additional spell damage and 425% mana regen. There are other properties of this that come from hero talents.
 Spellslinger
Spellslinger changes  Arcane Surge in one major way,  Splinterstorm in Midnight causes your Surge hit to generate 8x  Arcane Splinters and doubles Splinter generation during its buff;  Archmage's Wrath also buffs Surge damage a decent chunk. Keep in mind that Splinters for Spellslinger are fuel for the fire, it's what makes Spellslinger work.
 Sunfury
Sunfury has a few ways that it alters  Arcane Surge. First, when you cast Surge, any  Spellfire Spheres you have are consumed and added to your Surge buff for the duration of Surge. Second, when Surge ends, you gain  Arcane Soul and  Ashes of Inspiration, enabling a powerful burst of damage, ultimately extending your burst window; there is also a talent, though we won't take it for now, that can extend Surge's window a couple seconds.  Arcane Soul is a major reason why we cast  Touch of the Magi late in our Surge window as Sunfury. Lastly, when you cast Surge, you spawn an  Arcane Phoenix, which, for every Spellfire Sphere you consumed, cast empowered spells and the bird will also cast regular spells too. The bird is really complex in how it works, it detects whether it's single target or AOE and has a different action list based on this, it can cast it's own version of the following spells:

THE SPELLQUEUE WINDOW
There is a large amount of misinformation out in the internet about what the spellqueue window is and how it works. I hope this short write up will help elaborate on this misunderstood topic.

The spellqueue window is a brief window (default 0.4 seconds long) that exist at the end of casted spells and the global cooldown (GCD) that enables you to press your next button and queue the spell up at the exact point that your current cast or GCD finishes. This allows you to have seamless casting, see this example from one of my logs:

The  Arcane Blasts begin exactly where the previous ones end. This minimizes downtime and without spellqueueing we would have gaps in our casting between spells; these gaps would add up overtime and result in significant losses, especially gaps during cooldown windows.

The above is not something people generally disagree with, the spellqueue window prevents gaps in casting, however, where some people make the misunderstanding is on how that specific window works. The mistaken recommendation is that people lower this window to some factor of their ping or as a response to queueing the wrong spell, most common are people lowering it to 0.1-0.2 seconds. The issue with this is that most people do not spam keys 5-10 times a second, which is what lowering the queue window to this point would require to maintain the same seamless benefit, while the 0.4 second default only requires 3 presses per second.

If this were the only issue and was solved by simply hitting buttons faster, I would say 'go for it' if you can sustain that during playtime, however, there is no benefit to lowering the window for the reasons above and so there is only risk here. The spellqueue window can be overwritten up until the spell that is queued begins; this means that lowering it because of ping or wrong spell queueing would be solved by simply just queueing the spell correctly as soon as possible. What is instead happening is that people are not queueing their spells, resulting in a gap of time where they then queue up the 'correct' feeling spell. The spellqueue window was added to the game to combat lag issues, the idea that people are trying to remove its value because of lag issues is comedy, and the people who will insist that it helps them not cast the wrong spells, especially during high haste, are just hitting the wrong buttons and breaking their queue to solve the issue rather than training themselves to hit the right button sooner.

Ultimately, this matters less for specializations that are resource starved or healers or tanks where the 'right' button press is often more important than uptime, it also matters less for the players at the top levels of gameplay, but it matters a lot for the vast majority of dps that we have seamless and consistent uptime. For this reason, I would highly recommend you leave this at the default 0.4 second value. I hope this explanation helps, but ultimately the real takeaway from this is that you should attempte to always have spells queued up properly for maximum uptime.

You can reset your spellqueue back to 400 with the code below, but be mindful that some addons will misguidedly set this lower for you.

/console SpellQueueWindow 400

ARCANE SURGE
 Arcane Surge is simple, you use it, it spends all of your mana, does a burst of damage that hits 5 targets, then square root damage beyond that.  Arcane Surge then buffs you for 15-seconds, granting 35% additional spell damage and 425% mana regen. There are other properties of this that come from hero talents.
 Spellslinger
Spellslinger changes  Arcane Surge in one major way,  Splinterstorm in Midnight causes your Surge hit to generate 8x  Arcane Splinters and doubles Splinter generation during its buff;  Archmage's Wrath also buffs Surge damage a decent chunk. Keep in mind that Splinters for Spellslinger are fuel for the fire, it's what makes Spellslinger work.
 Sunfury
Sunfury has a few ways that it alters  Arcane Surge. First, when you cast Surge, any  Spellfire Spheres you have are consumed and added to your Surge buff for the duration of Surge. Second, when Surge ends, you gain  Arcane Soul and  Ashes of Inspiration, enabling a powerful burst of damage, ultimately extending your burst window; there is also a talent, though we won't take it for now, that can extend Surge's window a couple seconds.  Arcane Soul is a major reason why we cast  Touch of the Magi late in our Surge window as Sunfury. Lastly, when you cast Surge, you spawn an  Arcane Phoenix, which, for every Spellfire Sphere you consumed, cast empowered spells and the bird will also cast regular spells too. The bird is really complex in how it works, it detects whether it's single target or AOE and has a different action list based on this, it can cast it's own version of the following spells:

OPTIMIZING DAMAGE AS ARCANE
Some key things to note in general about good gameplay that will make you stand out;
Optimize around  Arcane Salvo; try to think about your next few seconds of gameplay, will adds spawn that need to die fast? Will the adds that are up now die soon? Consider saving and not saving around the current conditions - more adds generally means more damage overall and to your main target, thanks to  Resonance.
Don't allow yourself to get frazzled with mechanics, when movement is required, try to react quickly and consider your  Shimmer path and destination, ensure that you use Stutter Stepping in conjunction with Shimmer to minimize your downtime when handling mechanics.
 Prismatic Barrier is a must-cast defensive. Mages are not as tanky as we used to be, and Barrier spells received a sizable buff to both their quality and to their cast time - they only require 0.5 seconds now to cast, so there's no excuse - you can't deal damage if you're dead!
 Alter Time is a strong defensive, but don't be afraid to also double tap it for mobility in the moment. This can help you keep up with classes that have much better sustained mobility.
Your cooldown window only needs about 18 seconds for  Spellslinger and a maximum of 26 seconds for  Sunfury, and 18s for your miniburns; don't hold your cooldowns for too long if you can help it, remember how short the cooldown on  Touch of the Magi and  Arcane Surge are!

THE SPELLQUEUE WINDOW
There is a large amount of misinformation out in the internet about what the spellqueue window is and how it works. I hope this short write up will help elaborate on this misunderstood topic.

The spellqueue window is a brief window (default 0.4 seconds long) that exist at the end of casted spells and the global cooldown (GCD) that enables you to press your next button and queue the spell up at the exact point that your current cast or GCD finishes. This allows you to have seamless casting, see this example from one of my logs:

The  Arcane Blasts begin exactly where the previous ones end. This minimizes downtime and without spellqueueing we would have gaps in our casting between spells; these gaps would add up overtime and result in significant losses, especially gaps during cooldown windows.

The above is not something people generally disagree with, the spellqueue window prevents gaps in casting, however, where some people make the misunderstanding is on how that specific window works. The mistaken recommendation is that people lower this window to some factor of their ping or as a response to queueing the wrong spell, most common are people lowering it to 0.1-0.2 seconds. The issue with this is that most people do not spam keys 5-10 times a second, which is what lowering the queue window to this point would require to maintain the same seamless benefit, while the 0.4 second default only requires 3 presses per second.

If this were the only issue and was solved by simply hitting buttons faster, I would say 'go for it' if you can sustain that during playtime, however, there is no benefit to lowering the window for the reasons above and so there is only risk here. The spellqueue window can be overwritten up until the spell that is queued begins; this means that lowering it because of ping or wrong spell queueing would be solved by simply just queueing the spell correctly as soon as possible. What is instead happening is that people are not queueing their spells, resulting in a gap of time where they then queue up the 'correct' feeling spell. The spellqueue window was added to the game to combat lag issues, the idea that people are trying to remove its value because of lag issues is comedy, and the people who will insist that it helps them not cast the wrong spells, especially during high haste, are just hitting the wrong buttons and breaking their queue to solve the issue rather than training themselves to hit the right button sooner.

Ultimately, this matters less for specializations that are resource starved or healers or tanks where the 'right' button press is often more important than uptime, it also matters less for the players at the top levels of gameplay, but it matters a lot for the vast majority of dps that we have seamless and consistent uptime. For this reason, I would highly recommend you leave this at the default 0.4 second value. I hope this explanation helps, but ultimately the real takeaway from this is that you should attempte to always have spells queued up properly for maximum uptime.

You can reset your spellqueue back to 400 with the code below, but be mindful that some addons will misguidedly set this lower for you.

/console SpellQueueWindow 400

Lastly, when you cast Surge, you spawn an  Arcane Phoenix, which, for every Spellfire Sphere you consumed, cast empowered spells and the bird will also cast regular spells too. The bird is really complex in how it works, it detects whether it's single target or AOE and has a different action list based on this, it can cast it's own version of the following spells:

THE SPELLQUEUE WINDOW
There is a large amount of misinformation out in the internet about what the spellqueue window is and how it works. I hope this short write up will help elaborate on this misunderstood topic.

The spellqueue window is a brief window (default 0.4 seconds long) that exist at the end of casted spells and the global cooldown (GCD) that enables you to press your next button and queue the spell up at the exact point that your current cast or GCD finishes. This allows you to have seamless casting, see this example from one of my logs:

The  Arcane Blasts begin exactly where the previous ones end. This minimizes downtime and without spellqueueing we would have gaps in our casting between spells; these gaps would add up overtime and result in significant losses, especially gaps during cooldown windows.

The above is not something people generally disagree with, the spellqueue window prevents gaps in casting, however, where some people make the misunderstanding is on how that specific window works. The mistaken recommendation is that people lower this window to some factor of their ping or as a response to queueing the wrong spell, most common are people lowering it to 0.1-0.2 seconds. The issue with this is that most people do not spam keys 5-10 times a second, which is what lowering the queue window to this point would require to maintain the same seamless benefit, while the 0.4 second default only requires 3 presses per second.

If this were the only issue and was solved by simply hitting buttons faster, I would say 'go for it' if you can sustain that during playtime, however, there is no benefit to lowering the window for the reasons above and so there is only risk here. The spellqueue window can be overwritten up until the spell that is queued begins; this means that lowering it because of ping or wrong spell queueing would be solved by simply just queueing the spell correctly as soon as possible. What is instead happening is that people are not queueing their spells, resulting in a gap of time where they then queue up the 'correct' feeling spell. The spellqueue window was added to the game to combat lag issues, the idea that people are trying to remove its value because of lag issues is comedy, and the people who will insist that it helps them not cast the wrong spells, especially during high haste, are just hitting the wrong buttons and breaking their queue to solve the issue rather than training themselves to hit the right button sooner.

Ultimately, this matters less for specializations that are resource starved or healers or tanks where the 'right' button press is often more important than uptime, it also matters less for the players at the top levels of gameplay, but it matters a lot for the vast majority of dps that we have seamless and consistent uptime. For this reason, I would highly recommend you leave this at the default 0.4 second value. I hope this explanation helps, but ultimately the real takeaway from this is that you should attempte to always have spells queued up properly for maximum uptime.

You can reset your spellqueue back to 400 with the code below, but be mindful that some addons will misguidedly set this lower for you.

/console SpellQueueWindow 400
